## Research leads for the next session

1. **Burst sizing** (operator idea): rest a bigger bid (e.g. $50) for a few hours until a
   programme clears Kalshi's $1 minimum, then repeat across more markets. What we already know:
   - **Fills take the whole order.** 24 of 30 fills on orders of 50+ contracts took 100% of it,
     at 100 to 500 contracts alike. Fill loss scales with bid size.
   - **Fills arrive about once per 150–160 resting market-hours** (mention 161h, YouTube 151h),
     at about $9 cost basis per $10 fill.
   - **Reward scales less than size.** Our share of the queue is s/(F+s), so 5× size earns
     about 4× at typical depth. On reward per unit of risk, blanket bursts look neutral to
     slightly worse.
   - **The upside is the $1 minimum.** 50 of 69 markets in paid events were each estimated
     under $1. 96 unpaid events summed about $39 estimated and $0 paid.
2. **Programme age** (the test that decides lead 1). §9.42 says a programme pays most in its
   first hours. Re-score the snapshots by programme age (extend the calibration script with
   age buckets). If early hours pay several times more per contract while fills stay at about
   1 per 150h, a short, bigger early burst wins on both reward and risk.
3. **One yardstick across all markets.** Paid c/h against fill c/h per market type ranks any
   incentive programme against the others. The all-markets scan can rank programmes by
   (estimate × calibration) against that type's fill cost per market-hour.
4. **Calibration per type.** One global 1.34 under-reads YouTube less than mention events.
   Calibrate per market type once each has 10 or more paid events.
5. **$1-minimum harvest.** Count the short or thin programmes whose total would land between
   about $0.30 and $1, which are forfeited today. They are the only population where burst
   size changes the outcome.
